# Design philosophy

UI/UX Developer. Tone and idea for the civic CMS. Visual contract: [design-system.md](design-system.md). Site IA: [web-design.md](web-design.md) (Front-end Architect). How the page **reads**: [editorial-guidelines.md](editorial-guidelines.md) (Content Editor).

```text
tone:           modern, welcoming, civic publisher, generous
idea:           official record, built for a person — answer first, archive on request
look:           house tokens, generous craft; not USAFacts magenta, cream, Aeonik, or wordmark
```

Tone is aligned with [USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/): **modern, welcoming, civic publisher, generous.** The kit is not. Copy and scan path stay in the editorial guidelines. This file is how that tone looks. Tokens and presentation pass live in the design system. Routes and nav live in web design. Neither file invents a second tone.

**Idea.** Public data belongs to the public; it was not built for the public. The page does the decoder-ring work and hands back a fact a person can land on. Answer first, archive on request. Trust is an unmissable bound answer with the producer next to it.

**Language.** A civic product with two voices, not one. Serif — IBM Plex Serif — states the record: the citizen question and every bound figure. Sans runs the desk around it: labels, byline, nav, chart type. Three grounds carry the page, not one flat card colour: warm stone paper underfoot, white for evidence, and a cool, iso-luminant **well** for the answer — once per screen, felt as a change of place rather than a jump in brightness. Navy stays the institutional accent; clay and slate keep it company as chart colour, never as costume. The well is the one generous, elevated thing on the page — its own radius, its own lift, one continuous band for the sentence, the figures, and the cite. Everything else stays quiet: evidence is flat and equal-weight, method is later. Not a filing cabinet, not a ministry PDF, not a magazine, not a passionate-nerd costume.

**Guidelines**

1. **The answer is the product.** Question → designed fact-lede → figures → evidence → method. The bound clause and the display figure are the first designed object.
2. **Invitation, then depth.** Start at a glance. Context sits with the fact. Full cards and methodology are available, not first. Checkability is proximity of a citizen cite.
3. **Generous, not bureaucratic.** Space, scale contrast, product cards. Dry figures that feel made for a person. Sparse, misaligned, or whispered facts fail. No search, chat, newsletter, or “get notified.”
4. **Ownable and checkable.** One house on every slice. A cropped chart still shows title, unit, geography, source, and holes — and does not look like a USAFacts chart.
5. **One well.** Lede, stats, cite, and the first chart share left and right edge. `--measure` is method prose, not a second template glued to the chart.

Do not hide a hole or a cite to look finished. Generous landing is required.
